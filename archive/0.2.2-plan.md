# 0.2.2 — A fifth generation: Scenes

## Selected lineages

The atlas follows eighteen genres in five columns. The last column, **Scenes**, adds four profiles without adding a branch. A solid edge marks one primary lineage in the atlas, not a sole origin. Dashed arrows point from an additional influence to the style it helped shape. Column depth is not a timeline.

| New profile | Primary parent | Additional influences |
| --- | --- | --- |
| House | Disco | Soul |
| Techno | Disco | Hip-hop, house |
| Drum and bass | Hip-hop | Funk |
| Grunge | Alternative rock | Heavy metal |

Each profile describes the local scene, instruments and techniques, and pairs a landmark recording with a piece to learn. A “Led to” section lists both primary offshoots and styles the current genre helped shape, including when it has no primary children. A genre with no recorded successors has no such section. Quick search puts exact names and aliases ahead of incidental keyword matches.

## Shape and sound

The map creates one heading and horizontal position per catalog depth. Cards fit the measured horizontal gap and vertical spacing between rows; close columns use name-only cards and hide heading numbers while retaining full genre names and dates in accessible labels and profiles. Filters leave the horizontal positions unchanged.

Each new profile has an original two-bar study. House places keyboard stabs between kicks, techno builds a repeated synth sequence over a steady kick, drum and bass sets a syncopated break against slower bass accents, and grunge layers power chords over a sparse drop-D figure. The club studies and the drum and bass study specify their kick and snare beats; other studies use a shared backbeat. Playback starts by user gesture only.

## Acceptance and manual pass

1. Open `index.html` over `file://`. At 320 px, a short desktop viewport and a wide desktop viewport, check for five headings, eighteen nodes, and no overlapping or clipped labels. Switch branch filters and confirm columns stay put.
2. Follow Disco → House → Techno via “Led to”, then return to House from “Also shaped by”. Open Drum and bass by pressing Enter on its full name in quick search. Open Grunge from the Rock filter.
3. Visit each new profile’s Sound and Listening tabs. Play each study and compare the house and techno kick patterns with the faster, syncopated drum and bass break and the grunge chords. Changing genres stops playback.
4. Confirm no external resource is requested at launch; Wikipedia is optional.
